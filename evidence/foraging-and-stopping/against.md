# Against: the case against the baseline

The evidence against stopping a review on an estimate of the unseen, and the controversies. None of it is removed to make the baseline look stronger.

## The estimator's assumptions fail for a search

- The estimators treat the log as a random sample of one population. A search is aimed, and each string targets a strand. This review's inference, untested here.
- Under unequal chances of finding a concept, Chao1 is a lower bound (Chao 1987, located only). It cannot promise that little is left.
- A concept is a reader's judgment. Two readers may count different concepts; no inter-rater study was read.
- Ranked results are not independent draws: the next result depends on the last. This review's inference.

## The studies read caution too

- The reliability of a remainder estimate depends on the model that ranks the records (Callaghan 2020, read).
- The Cochrane Handbook does not recommend acting on such estimates as of mid-2023, since stopping rules need more validation (Lefebvre 2025, read).
- Heuristic stops are inconsistent (Callaghan 2020), yet cheap and readable. The estimator needs a log and a floor of results.

## Evidence from this pass

- The family's logs hold no counts of results with new concepts, so the estimator has no real log to run on ([on the family](on-the-family.md)).
- In the simulation, a sample of two equal results has no singleton and looks complete. The estimator needs a floor, set at 30 results, a PROVISIONAL value ([baseline](baseline.md)).
- The two published forms of Chao1 give different estimates when doubletons are few, so a result is only as comparable as the form named.

## Controversies

- Whether estimators of the remainder should replace human judgment of when to stop is open in review practice (Lefebvre 2025).
- Whether species estimators transfer to documents is untested; the documents of a review are not exchangeable draws.
- No failed replication was found, and none was looked for in this first pass.
